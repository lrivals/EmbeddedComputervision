# Latency bound of the accelerator and the post-processing (M30, T30.0, T30.1)

Goal: a latency bound that can be **demonstrated**, not a mean (sixth gap of the ARCYS state
of the art). Generated by `python tools/perf_model.py --wcet` (2026-10-20). The bound only
reads `model/<net>/manifest.json` and the kernel configuration
(`hls/kernels/accel_config.hpp`). It never reads an image.

## Argument: the accelerator does not depend on the image

The engine runs a fixed schedule of `LayerDesc` descriptors. In `hls/kernels/conv_pe.cpp`
(`conv_layer`, `conv_tile`, `compute`) and `hls/kernels/output_stage.hpp` (`store_tile`):

- **Loop bounds**: every trip count is computed from descriptor fields (`k, pad, cin, cout,
  h, w, pool_k, pool_s, tr, tc, fold`) and the tile constants (`TM, TN, TRB, TCB, RQ`).
- **Data**: activations and weights are only operands. They are never a loop or branch
  condition.
  - Requantization, leaky and saturation are straight-line code.
  - The max-pool is a compare over a fixed window.
  - The partial last tiles are fixed by the layer shape.

So the cycle count of a conv is a function of its descriptor only, as the C-sim counter
`count_cycles` and its Python copy `layer_cycles` already assume.
`python/tests/test_perf_model.py` checks the copy layer by layer against `make hls-cycles`.

### What the bound adds to the cycle model

The model's `overlapped` count is what the ping-pong schedule should achieve. The bound
(`wcet_layer`) gives up every overlap and adds what the C-sim does not model:

| Term | Value | Why it is an upper bound |
|---|---|---|
| `sequential` | loads + compute + store of every tile, one after the other | the overlapped schedule can only be shorter |
| DDR | bytes on the m_axi ports / 13.44 GB/s (`DDR_BW`) | every byte at the effective KV260 bandwidth, nothing hidden |
| latency | 64 cycles per m_axi phase (2 loads per input-channel tile + 1 store per tile) | **assumption**: bursts within a phase are issued back to back (Vitis default of 16 outstanding requests); to be replaced by the T30.2 measurement |

The DDR and latency terms are the only ones that are not cycle-exact. They depend on the
transfer sizes (descriptor only) and on the board, not on the image.

## Post-processing (`yolo_post`): the data-dependent part

The number of cells above the t_o threshold, of candidates (cell × class above the score
threshold) and of occupied NMS slots depends on the image. `postproc_wcet` takes the worst
case of each:

| Term | Bound |
|---|---|
| tables | 256 per head |
| scan | one cycle per (anchor, cell) |
| decode | every cell survives: 4 + C reads, + 2C for the v2 softmax, + C scores |
| candidates | every cell × every class whose score can pass the threshold. Sigmoid heads: C. Softmax heads: the Q16 scores of a cell sum to at most 2¹⁶ + C/2, so at most ⌊(2¹⁶ + C/2)/(conf_q + 1)⌋ pass (4 at 0.25, all 20 at 0.005) |
| NMS | per candidate, the two loops of `Selector::push` over the 256 slots (2 · CAP) |
| store | 6 words for each of the 256 slots |

### Reached in simulation (T30.1, adversarial inputs)

`tb_post --csv build/hls/cycles_post.csv` (in `make hls-cycles`) adds the input "every cell
and every class passes" (t_o = t_c = 127, small boxes spread over the image).
`test_postproc_bound_vs_adversarial_csim` checks every row against the bound.

| Network, case | Candidates (bound) | C-sim cycles | Static bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| v2, all cells, random scores, conf 0.25 | 1,181 (3,380) | 342,375 | 1,804,179 |
| v2, all classes, conf 0.005 | 16,900 (16,900) | 4,367,124 | 8,726,419 |
| v3, all classes, conf 0.25 | 202,800 (202,800) | 52,304,230 | 104,253,925 |

- **Reached terms**: the tables, scan and decode terms are reached exactly, and on v3 so is the
  number of candidates.
- **NMS gap**: the C-sim counter charges one slot loop per candidate, the bound charges both,
  hence the factor ≈ 2 on the NMS.
- **YOLOv4-tiny and YOLOv7-tiny**: their heads (`scale_x_y`, `new_coords`) are not supported
  by `yolo_post`. Their post-processing stays on the ARM and is not bounded here. Their row
  below still counts the hardware post-processing bound of a two-head 80-class network, as an
  order of magnitude.

### Finding: the NMS dominates the worst case of the sigmoid heads

With 80 independent sigmoids, one cell can produce 80 candidates. The unsorted NMS then costs
2 × 256 compares per candidate: 0.52 s per frame in the worst case, against 77 ms for the
whole accelerator. Real images are far from it (1 to 60 candidates, `results/postproc_hw.md`),
but a watchdog sized on the bound would be useless. A demonstrable bound needs a cap on the
candidates; this is a design input for M29/M31, not done here:

- keep the best class only per cell (C → 1: 2,535 candidates on v3, 6.5 ms);
- or stop the stream after N candidates, with the overflow flag already in `res[1]`.

## Watchdog for T29.5

The watchdog value is the frame bound × 1.25, rounded up to the next ms. It is printed
above per network:

- **Tiny-YOLOv2** (softmax, conf 0.25): 116 ms, a usable value. The accelerator bound
  (83 ms) is 2.2 × the overlapped model (37 ms).
- **v3 and v4**: their value is dominated by the uncapped NMS (finding above). They need the
  candidate cap before the watchdog is meaningful.
